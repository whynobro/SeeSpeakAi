# Feedback session guide (video call)

Owner: Holly. For the Saturday 6 PM call with our first user, and any later call. About 25 minutes. We ask for feedback. We do not test people.

## Before the call (by 5:30 PM)

- [ ] Michael: a working link (production or a preview) that opens on a phone and a laptop. Send it to Holly.
- [ ] One of us runs the whole flow on that link: hearing check, 5 words, progress screen.
- [ ] Decide who talks (Holly) and who takes notes (one person, camera on, mostly silent).
- [ ] Ask ahead, by message: "Do you prefer captions, chat, an interpreter, or voice on the call?" Turn on live captions either way.
- [ ] Have `user-tests.md` open to fill in.

## How the app works over a call

- They open the link on **their own phone or laptop**. The app listens through their own microphone, on their device. The call does not carry the sound to us and does not need to.
- They share their screen, or hold the phone up to the camera, so we can see the meter and the result.
- If the call software and the app fight over the microphone, they mute themselves in the call while they say a word, then unmute to talk.
- Headphones in the call can stay on.

## Things we do not know yet, so we ask and do not assume

- How they describe their own hearing, and whether they use hearing aids, an implant or neither.
- Whether they use speech, sign or both, and whether spoken English practice is something they would ever choose.
- Which name or label they want in our notes and our pitch.

If they are a hearing ASL learner and not a hard-of-hearing speaker, the session still helps: we ask how the idea and the wording land with them and with deaf and hard-of-hearing people they know, and we do not present them as our hard-of-hearing user.

## Questions

1. What did you expect to happen that did not?
2. Did any result feel wrong? Which word?
3. Was the meter clear without sound? What about the result screen?
4. Is there any wording that felt like it was about "fixing" you, or that you would change?
5. Would you use this on your own? When? What would stop you?
6. What would make it worth using a second time?
7. Who do you think this is for? Who is it not for?
8. If your school or a speech therapist offered this for free, would you take it? Would you pay for it yourself?
9. How do you feel about the sign cards idea: sign shown next to practice words, as a setting?
10. What should we never say when we present this tomorrow?
