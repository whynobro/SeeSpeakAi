# Judge answers

Hard questions we expect, with the short answer to say out loud and the source behind it. Every number here
has a source. Do not round up, combine numbers, or add ones that are not here. Longer notes are in
[`docs/research.md`](../research.md).

## "They can hear a little. Why not just have the app say the word?"

**Say:** "They have heard 'sip' and 'ship' thousands of times. The part they miss is the 's' itself. It sits at 4
to 8 kHz, the pitch range hearing loss usually takes first, so a louder word does not bring it back. Their 's' can
drift toward 'sh', 'sip' toward 'ship', and they can't hear their own 's' to catch it. The app shows their own
sound on screen. That's the feedback they're missing."

Why this holds up:

- "s" energy sits around 4 to 8 kHz, "sh" around 2 to 4 kHz ([BATOD, Ling Six](https://www.batod.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/The-Ling-6-Sound-Test.pdf)).
- How well a child hears at 4 kHz through hearing aids predicts how well they say "s" endings
  ([Koehlinger 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4398614)).
- As high-pitch hearing gets worse, speakers' "s" drifts lower, toward "sh"
  ([Pittman 2018](https://doi.org/10.1044/2018_JSLHR-H-17-0460), abstract only).
- In children with cochlear implants, "sh" is the most common sound said in place of "s", about 24% of their "s"
  errors; hearing children do the reverse (Blamey 2001, cited in
  [Li, Bunta and Tomblin 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5831615)). Secondhand, and 24% of
  errors, not of children: keep it for questions.
- Children with cochlear implants make a less distinct "s" versus "sh" contrast
  ([Reidy 2017](https://hesp.umd.edu/sites/hesp.umd.edu/files/pubs/reidyetal2017.pdf)).
- Check Pittman 2018's author order before putting it on a slide: one listing shows a different first author.

## "Then why not lower the pitch of 's' so they can hear it?"

**Say:** "Hearing aids already try that. In a randomized trial of 44 three-year-olds it did not improve
language, and consonant scores came out slightly lower. So we don't rely on hearing the sound. We show it."

- [Ching 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24350695/), Int J Audiol 52 Suppl 2: frequency compression,
  44 children aged 3, no significant change in language; consonant scores lower, more "ch" said as "sh".
  Read through a summary, not the full paper.
- Do NOT say it made children "slower" or "worse at talking". It found no gain in language and a small drop in
  consonant scores.
- Possible reason (our reading, not the authors' stated conclusion): squeezing the high pitches into a
  narrower low band pushes "s" and "sh" closer together, so they get harder to tell apart. If asked, say "one
  likely reason", not "the reason".
- A smaller study found lowering helps children hear high sounds in quiet, not in noise, and did not test
  speaking ([Wolfe 2010](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/pdf/10.3766/jaaa.21.10.2.pdf)).

## "Why not use speech recognition to check the word?"

**Say:** "It gets about half of deaf speakers' words wrong, and it 'autocorrects' a missing 's'. We measure
the hiss itself."

- Word error rate 52.6% for 24 deaf and hard-of-hearing adults vs 5.0% for 9 hearing adults, across four
  systems ([Zhao 2024](https://koenecke.infosci.cornell.edu/files/Laryngoscope_Zhao2024.pdf)).

## "How many people is this for?"

- 2 to 3 of every 1,000 US children are born with detectable hearing loss
  ([NIDCD](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/statistics/quick-statistics-hearing), CDC data 1999 to 2007).
- 14.9% of US children aged 6 to 19 had at least slight hearing loss in one or both ears
  ([Niskar, JAMA 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9546565/), data 1988 to 1994). Mostly slight and in
  one ear: say "slight hearing loss", never "deaf".
- 34 million children worldwide have disabling hearing loss, of 430 million people
  ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/deafness-and-hearing-loss)).
- 70,000 US students aged 3 to 21 were served for hearing impairment under special education law in 2022-23
  ([NCES](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/d23/tables/dt23_204.30.asp)).

## "Why practice between sessions?"

- Hard-of-hearing elementary students who see a school speech therapist average 1.7 sessions a week of about
  31 minutes. Parents said 30% got no school services at all
  ([Page 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6430501)).
- The median school speech therapist has 50 students
  ([ASHA 2026 Schools Survey](https://www.asha.org/siteassets/surveys/2026-schools-survey-slp-caseload.pdf)).

## Do not say

- "Deaf lisp" or "lisp". We found no source for it. Say "the 's' can drift toward 'sh'".
- That deaf children "say ship". Say "can drift toward".
- A percent of hard-of-hearing children with "s" errors. We found no study that gives one.
- A private speech therapy price per session. We found only blog numbers.
- That the app improves speech. No trial exists yet. Our own numbers are in `user-tests.md`.
