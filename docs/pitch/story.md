## Decision (Holly, Sat Nov 14)

Our first user is a Cal Poly student reached through the Cal Poly ASL Club. They agreed to a video call on Saturday at 6 PM. So the age group for tuning is **college-age adult**. We do not write their name here until they say how they want to be named, and we do not describe their hearing until they tell us (see `zoom-session-guide.md`).

That makes this choice B for the live story, with choice A as where the problem starts and where the money is. The two sentences below are a draft until the call.

- **The user, in one sentence:** a first-year Cal Poly student with hearing aids who got speech practice at school until graduation, and now has none.
- **The story, in one sentence:** the "s" is too high for them to hear, so they cannot tell if they said it, and SeeSpeak shows them on their own phone.

If a parent and child agree to try the app by Saturday night, switch to choice A and tell Michael at once: the tuning changes.

## Lines we can say

- "If you cannot hear a sound, you cannot copy it."
- "In one study, when a sentence needed the 's' verb ending, as in 'she runs', 6-year-olds who are hard of hearing used it 75% of the time and hearing 6-year-olds 97% of the time." (Koehlinger 2013)
- "School speech therapy is about two half-hour sessions a week, and the therapist has 50 students." (Page 2018: 1.7 sessions of about 31 minutes; ASHA 2026: median of 50 students)
- "Speech practice apps exist, but none we found builds the practice from your own hearing."

## Lines we must not say

- "First speech app for deaf learners."
- "The AI listens to your speech."
- Anything about "fixing" speech or hearing.
- Any number for dropped "s" endings at ages 8 to 12 or at college age. We found no study that measures it.
